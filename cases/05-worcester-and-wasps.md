# A home fixture with six days’ cash

Chapter case 05 · Chapter 5, *Budgets, cash and financial control* · England

## The public record

In its January 2023 report on the loss of Worcester Warriors and Wasps from the Premiership, the House of Commons Digital, Culture, Media and Sport Committee examined missed warning signs and the division of oversight between rugby’s authorities. It recorded late salary reports at Worcester and the financial burden of Wasps’ move to Coventry. The competition discarded the departing clubs’ results from the league table. Written evidence from the Rugby Football Union and Premiership Rugby also described steps to maintain the two under-18 academies and keep women’s teams playing. One financial failure therefore reached several entities whose obligations and resources differed.

- [House of Commons Digital, Culture, Media and Sport Committee, Current issues in rugby union, HC 1018, 17 January 2023, paras 1–5](https://publications.parliament.uk/pa/cm5803/cmselect/cmcumeds/1018/report.html)
- [Rugby Football Union and Premiership Rugby, written evidence to the Committee, 6 December 2022, sections on roles and support for players](https://committees.parliament.uk/writtenevidence/114194/pdf/)

## Your brief

The following scenario, roles, records and deadlines are constructed for teaching.

You are finance director of one of City Women’s League’s six semi-professional clubs in this fictional exercise. A home fixture is six days away. The owner promises a transfer by Friday, while players have already asked the union whether wages will arrive. The league needs a decision in time to release the visiting team’s travel booking.

| Exercise record | What you have received |
| --- | --- |
| Tuesday’s reconciled bank balance | £46,000 cleared, of which £24,000 is an unspent grant restricted to junior coaching. There is no borrowing facility and no grant variation. |
| Committed payments | £17,000 for player and staff payroll is due Thursday, £8,000 to the stadium operator on Friday and £3,000 to the match-day medical provider on Friday. Assume all three sums fall due even if the fixture is postponed unless the supplier agrees otherwise. |
| Expected receipts | The owner’s email anticipates £10,000. There is no binding commitment or cleared transfer. £7,000 of ticket sales would clear two days after the match if it goes ahead. The club has sold advance tickets for the fixture. |
| League procedure | The club must notify the league immediately of a credible risk that a fixture cannot be fulfilled. The stadium operator can reschedule payment by written variation. The medical provider will supply no cover on credit. Away travel becomes non-refundable on Wednesday at noon. |

## Decisions due

1. Complete the next two weeks of the chapter’s 13-week ledger under a cleared-cash case and a case in which the owner pays on time. Show restricted cash, receipts against dates, due commitments and the first point at which the general balance fails.
2. Prepare a recommendation before Wednesday noon. Name what the club board, the competition director and each supplier can authorise, identify the professional advice needed if debts may go unpaid and specify who can stop additional ticket sales.
3. Draft a notice to the visiting club, players and ticket holders with separate time commitments for each. Describe a route by which junior coaching could continue if the senior fixture is lost, including who controls the grant.

## An agent assignment for the finance director

Have the agent reconcile bank activity, restricted grants and open orders to a weekly cash forecast. An exception belongs on the officer’s desk when a proposed payment would consume ring-fenced money, even if the combined account can comfortably clear it.
