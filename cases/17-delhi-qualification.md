# A qualifier a delegation cannot enter

Cross-department simulation 17 · Chapter 10, *High performance and athlete pathways* · India and international Olympic sport

## The public record

In February 2019 two Pakistani athletes and an official could not obtain Indian entry visas in time for the ISSF World Cup in New Delhi. The IOC Executive Board withdrew Olympic qualifying status from the men’s 25 m rapid fire pistol event and asked the international federation to propose how the two quota places should be reassigned. Its decision also suspended discussions with India about future hosting applications until the government gave written guarantees. Removing qualifying status from one event and setting a condition for future hosts were separate decisions.

- [International Olympic Committee, decision on the New Delhi qualification event, 22 February 2019](https://www.olympics.com/ioc/news/ioc-executive-board-revokes-olympic-qualification-status-of-men-s-25m-rapid-fire-pistol-event-in-new-delhi)
- [International Shooting Sport Federation, notice on the two quota places, 22 February 2019](https://www.issf-sports.org/news/3248)

## Your brief

The following scenario, roles, records and deadlines are constructed for teaching.

In this fictional transfer, Harbour Sport Federation hosts an international qualifying regatta. One visiting crew risks missing Thursday’s entries deadline because its visa applications remain undecided. As host general secretary, you have thirty minutes to brief the international federation, the delegation and the board chair. Each expects a different remedy.

| Exercise record | What you have received |
| --- | --- |
| Delegation call, Monday 09.00 | The team manager confirms that four applications were received twelve days ago. The host liaison has a receipt for each. The decision field on each receipt is empty. The athletes have authorised the liaison to discuss application status with the designated government office. |
| Qualifying agreement, clause 8 | The international federation approves changes to qualifying status, deadlines and replacement events. Harbour can change local accreditation times. The agreement designates a separate government contact for entry decisions. |
| Capacity option | A regatta three weeks later could stage the event, subject to written approval. Two smaller federations report that an extra journey would exceed their travel grants. A sponsor has already invited guests to a medal presentation. |

## Decisions due

1. Draw up a decision paper distinguishing entry permission, local accreditation and the competition’s qualifying status. Name the official who can act on each, the evidence that official needs and the latest meaningful decision time.
2. Compare a short extension, local competition with qualifying status reserved, and an alternative qualifier. Show who bears additional travel costs and how other eligible crews would be heard before a material change.
3. Give a read-only AI agent the task of reconciling the four application receipts with the delegation list. Specify its permitted fields and a human correction route. Draft separate messages for the affected delegation and the public.
