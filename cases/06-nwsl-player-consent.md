# Two clubs agree a fee. Who can move the player?

Chapter case 06 · Chapter 6, *Leagues, markets and media rights* · United States

## The public record

The National Women’s Soccer League and its players’ association announced a new collective bargaining agreement on 22 August 2024, extending their agreement through 2030. Section 13.11 of the published agreement governs trades and transfers between league clubs. From the 2025 season, each requires the player’s freely given written consent when the transaction is executed. A player moving within the season ordinarily has 48 hours to report, although the acquiring team can allow a later date. The agreement also provides two consecutive days to organise personal affairs. The timing of consent and the acquiring club’s discretion over reporting each have practical consequences for the transaction.

- [National Women’s Soccer League and NWSL Players Association, Collective Bargaining Agreement 2024–2030, section 13.11, printed p. 47](https://www.nwslplayers.com/_files/ugd/84dade_0b675d2ffb2048f8b89257555a35f231.pdf)
- [National Women’s Soccer League, announcement of the collective bargaining agreement, 22 August 2024](https://www.nwslsoccer.com/news/nwsl-and-nwslpa-agree-to-historic-collective-bargaining-agreement)

## Your brief

The following scenario, roles, records and deadlines are constructed for teaching.

In this fictional US league, the six clubs have adopted the wording of section 13.11 for moves within their competition. As registrar, you receive a request at 14.00 on the final day of registration. The clubs want you to clear the move in time for a 17.00 broadcast. The player has an existing contract and is still preparing for her own club’s Saturday fixture.

| Exercise record | What you have received |
| --- | --- |
| Terms between the clubs | The clubs have agreed a US$25,000 fee, payable on effective registration. The window closes at 18.00. The league’s rules give the registrar no discretion to extend it. |
| Player correspondence | Last month the player wrote that she would consider a move closer to home. At 13.20 today she requested time to speak privately with her representative and asked which club would pay two weeks of overlapping rent. |
| Receiving club’s proposal | The club can approve US$1,800 towards the overlap. It has booked a medical appointment for the following morning and wants the player at training then. No reporting date has been agreed with her. |
| Communications | A sponsor has scheduled a reveal at 17.00. Both clubs have draft graphics naming the player. The registrar holds neither her written consent to this transaction nor permission to announce it. |

## Decisions due

1. Draft the registrar’s instruction to the clubs at 14.15. Specify who can agree the rent allowance and reporting time, who can withhold consent, and what goes in the registration file.
2. Prepare two versions of the closing entry for 18.00, one for a completed transfer and one if consent has not arrived. Allocate responsibility for the fee, roster, travel and public announcement in each.
3. Write the sporting director’s response if the player declines. Preserve the existing competition arrangements and explain how she can raise any pressure to accept without going through the negotiator.

## An agent assignment for the league registrar

Test a transfer agent on an agreed fee with consent still outstanding. It may calculate the window and notify the registrar of a missing item. The player must be able to communicate a decision privately and see how that decision changes registration.
