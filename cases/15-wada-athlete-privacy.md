# The first call after an athlete’s medical record becomes public

Chapter case 15 · Chapter 15, *Data, climate and operational risk* · Canada and international sport

## The public record

Following the 2016 Rio Olympics, personal records of 127 athletes were published after unauthorised access to the World Anti-Doping Agency’s information system. The Canadian Privacy Commissioner distinguished information known to have been published from other information that might have been compromised. The records included therapeutic use exemptions as well as test information. On 7 February 2018 the Commissioner found the complaint well founded and conditionally resolved, and entered into a compliance agreement with WADA. The Commissioner’s 2018–19 annual report subsequently confirmed that WADA had implemented the recommendations to the regulator’s satisfaction.

- [Office of the Privacy Commissioner of Canada, ‘Breach of the World Anti-Doping database’, PIPEDA Report of Findings 2018-006, 7 February 2018, paras 35–38, 87–88](https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/opc-actions-and-decisions/investigations/investigations-into-businesses/2018/pipeda-2018-006/)
- [Office of the Privacy Commissioner of Canada, Annual Report 2018–19, ‘World Anti-Doping Agency (WADA) completes implementation of investigation recommendations’](https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/opc-actions-and-decisions/ar_index/201819/ar_201819/)

## Your brief

The following scenario, roles, records and deadlines are constructed for teaching.

In this fictional desk exercise, a Canadian tournament federation learns that a journalist has obtained parts of two athletes’ medical exemption forms. You are the federation’s privacy lead. The organiser and a contracted event service both held copies for different purposes, and teams are about to depart.

| Exercise record | What you have received |
| --- | --- |
| Confirmed | Two athletes privately confirm that the extracts are theirs. Neither has agreed to be named. A journalist seeks comment before 18.00. |
| Unresolved | A service provider held files for 14 athletes. It can report tomorrow which files the event desk requested. Possession of a file does not establish who disclosed it. |
| Responsibilities | The federation privacy lead coordinates the incident. Its medical liaison can contact athletes through a previously agreed channel. The provider contract requires prompt notice to the federation and preservation of records. |
| Calendar | The team doctor travels at 16.00. A deputy is available. The organiser proposes announcing that only two athletes were affected. |

## Decisions due

1. Prepare an incident record with three columns for verified facts, outstanding questions and the owner of each enquiry. Identify who preserves the records, assesses notification duties and logs the reasons and timing.
2. Draft two messages: a private first contact that offers support and a time for the next update, and a short public response which neither identifies an athlete nor comments on compliance with anti-doping rules.
3. Draw up a handover for the travelling doctor and a board assurance agenda for the following week. Include evidence that a provider action was completed and a route for athletes to correct inaccurate information.

## An agent assignment for the data protection officer

Give an incident agent a minimal record of affected processes, owners and notification clocks. It can chase receipt of the action list while clinicians contact affected athletes. Distribute the status of the response without circulating the underlying medical files.
