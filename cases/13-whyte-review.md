# When the person who selects the team hears complaints

Chapter case 13 · Chapter 13, *Safeguarding, integrity and welfare* · United Kingdom

## The public record

Sport England and UK Sport commissioned Anne Whyte QC to investigate mistreatment in gymnastics. Her report, published on 16 June 2022, documents how some gymnasts and parents feared that raising a concern would cost a gymnast coaching opportunities or selection. It also examines what British Gymnastics could learn from complaints handled at club level. The two funding bodies’ January 2023 response contained 19 commitments covering workforce support, athlete support, governance, dispute resolution and safer sporting environments. The history allows administrators to examine how case information reaches a funder or board while protecting the child who supplied it.

## Your brief

The following scenario, roles, records and deadlines are constructed for teaching.

In this fictional English club exercise, a junior athlete tells a volunteer that a coach has repeatedly required injured children to continue a drill. The athlete fears losing the weekend squad place. The head coach appoints both the assistant coaches and the selectors. The duty manager must arrange safe supervision before the next session.

| Exercise record | What you have received |
| --- | --- |
| Club policy | The duty manager can remove an adult from a session pending review. The independent safeguarding lead handles referrals and must record the reason, recipient and time of any disclosure. |
| Conflict declaration | The club welfare officer is the head coach’s spouse. The federation’s safeguarding officer can receive direct referrals. The club has current contact details for local authority children’s services and the designated officer for allegations concerning adults working with children. |
| Programme board | Another qualified coach can take tonight’s session. The selection meeting is tomorrow morning. Two earlier messages about the same drill were filed as ‘training feedback’. |
| Information from the family | The athlete wants a trusted adult at the next conversation and asks whether the head coach will see the report before the weekend. |

## Decisions due

1. Write the duty manager’s handover for the next hour. Record the athlete’s words accurately, the immediate welfare arrangements, the independent recipient and the rationale for any statutory referral or consultation. Address imminent danger separately.
2. Draft a temporary staffing and selection direction. Name the person who can review it and set a time. Give the athlete a contact outside the coaching chain and explain what can be shared.
3. Prepare a board assurance note identifying how to examine the two previous messages and monitor missed referrals, retaliation and time to follow-up without circulating identifiable case files.

## An agent assignment for the designated safeguarding lead

Deploy an administrative agent to confirm that an authorised recipient acknowledged a referral, with a separate route when that officer is implicated. Limit routine logs to case identifiers and timestamps. Personal testimony stays with trained people who can support and hear the athlete.
