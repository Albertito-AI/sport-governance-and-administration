# What does a replacement sponsor actually buy?

Chapter case 07 · Chapter 7, *Supporters, marketing and sponsorship* · Australia

## The public record

On 22 October 2022 Hancock Prospecting announced that it had withdrawn its proposed partnership with Netball Australia. Its statement offered four months of interim sponsorship if accepted and also addressed Roy Hill’s separate arrangement in Western Australia. On 31 October the Victorian Premier announced an A$15 million partnership between Netball Australia and Visit Victoria through June 2027. It included branding on the Diamonds’ playing kit, tourism promotion by players and coaches, five Tests and high-performance camps in Victoria between 2023 and 2026, and the 2023 Super Netball Grand Final in the state. The announced rights show how directly a commercial negotiation can shape the competition calendar and the work of athletes.

- [Premier of Victoria, Australian Netball to Showcase Victoria to the World, 31 October 2022](https://www.premier.vic.gov.au/australian-netball-showcase-victoria-world)

## Your brief

The following scenario, roles, records and deadlines are constructed for teaching.

An Australian women’s league in this simulation has lost an unsigned commercial offer. You are its commercial director. A regional tourism authority offers A$120,000 for a season and wants the chair to announce the agreement tomorrow. The board can sell league-owned inventory. Each participating club controls its home court and its own players’ individual appearances.

| Exercise record | What you have received |
| --- | --- |
| Proposed rights | The buyer asks for shirt-front branding at six clubs, a named grand final hosted in its state, and six individual player appearances. The final venue is already granted to the top seed under the competition rules. |
| Existing inventory | Two clubs have sold their shirt fronts. The league controls the digital scoreboard at its finals. Its broadcast agreement allows brand identification on that scoreboard subject to the broadcaster’s written approval. |
| Costs and cash | The buyer offers A$30,000 on signature and A$90,000 after an accepted delivery report. The league estimates A$24,000 in production and A$18,000 in travel for the original package. Its next payroll of A$38,000 falls in ten days, against A$21,000 in available unrestricted cash. |
| Player representative’s note | The association will put optional appearances to players if the league supplies dates, fees, image-use terms and a contact for raising objections privately. No player has yet agreed to appear. |

## Decisions due

1. Produce a rights schedule for the board. For each requested asset name its owner, any prior grant, the approval still needed, an available substitute and the price or evidence needed to value it.
2. Submit a cash and fulfilment note. Show when the first instalment would cover payroll, what the package contributes after estimated direct costs, and what changes if the delivery report is disputed. Specify who checks cash before payment is promised.
3. Draft a counteroffer and a short holding statement. Give the tourism authority exposure it can verify, reserve the venue decision to the proper body, and give players a practical way to decline an individual appearance.

## An agent assignment for the commercial director

Use an inventory agent to price the rights displaced by a replacement sponsor, including appearances and staff time. The athlete representative confirms who agreed to wear or endorse what. The director has to explain any undertaking that exceeds the league’s own inventory.
