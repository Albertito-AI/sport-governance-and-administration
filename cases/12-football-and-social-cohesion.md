# What survived the final whistle in Iraq?

Chapter case 12 · Chapter 12, *Sport for development and the limits of evidence* · Iraq

## The public record

Salma Mousa’s field experiment in northern Iraq assigned displaced Christian amateur footballers to teams with Muslim players or to all-Christian teams. Compared with the latter group, Christians assigned Muslim team-mates were more willing to register for another mixed team and to train with Muslims later. They were also more likely to vote for a Muslim player from a different team for a sportsmanship award. Evidence of changes in attitudes and behaviour towards strangers away from football was much weaker. The distinction matters to a funder choosing between a sporting participation objective and a claim about relations across a whole district.

- [Salma Mousa, ‘Building social cohesion between Christians and Muslims through soccer in post-ISIS Iraq’ (2020) 369 Science 866–870, doi:10.1126/science.abb3153, full article](https://poverty-action.org/sites/default/files/publications/Building-social-cohesion-between-Christians-and-Muslims-through-soccer-in-post-ISIS-Iraq.pdf)
- [Abdul Latif Jameel Poverty Action Lab, ‘Inter-religious Soccer Leagues to Promote Social Cohesion in Post-ISIS Iraq’, evaluation and outcome measures](https://www.povertyactionlab.org/evaluation/inter-religious-soccer-leagues-promote-social-cohesion-post-isis-iraq)

## Your brief

The following scenario, roles, records and deadlines are constructed for teaching.

In this fictional exercise Borough Sport Trust seeks renewal of a mixed-neighbourhood league. The municipality wants a sentence for its public budget submission tomorrow. Community organisers worry that a claim about citywide reconciliation will put pressure on players to disclose personal beliefs.

| Exercise record | What you have received |
| --- | --- |
| Attendance register | Of 96 initial participants, 68 finished the season. The register covers matches, age group and attendance. It contains no question about social attitudes. |
| Return bookings | The following term, 34 of the original 96 signed up for another mixed team. Twenty others supplied no follow-up contact details. |
| Feedback cards | Of 39 players who returned anonymous cards at the final, 31 would play with the same team again. The cards cannot be linked to registrations. No comparison group was recruited. |
| Funder’s copy | The draft line reads, ‘Our league reconciled the two neighbourhoods’. Two resident associations are willing to design an affordable follow-up with participants and non-participants. |

## Decisions due

1. Give the treasurer a defensible budget sentence and a footnote with the relevant numerator, denominator and missing observations. Explain which records cannot be combined.
2. Design two measures for the next grant, one about a choice made in sport and one about an everyday choice outside it. Specify who defines the measure, who can decline and what a comparison would show.
3. Send the board a one-page renewal recommendation. Cost the evaluation in staff time, identify an alternative if comparison sites decline, and give the resident associations a say over public wording.

## An agent assignment for the programme evaluator

Ask an evaluation agent to trace each outcome to an observation, respondent group and follow-up interval. Test its draft against a group that changed teams and a participant who stopped attending. The commissioner decides what claim survives those gaps.
