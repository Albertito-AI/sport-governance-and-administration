# Who answers for admission to a final?

Chapter case 08 · Chapter 8, *Facilities, procurement and events* · France and European football

## The public record

The independent panel reviewing the UEFA Champions League final played at the Stade de France on 28 May 2022 concluded that UEFA, as event owner, bore primary responsibility for the organisational failures. It recognised the roles of the French federation and the police, while tracing duties of supervision and coordination back to UEFA. On 7 March 2023 UEFA announced a refund scheme covering all 19,618 tickets in Liverpool’s allocation. Liverpool had sold those tickets and agreed to administer refunds, with UEFA reimbursing the club. The scheme also provided for eligible spectators who had bought from UEFA and for holders of accessibility tickets and tickets for their companions.

- [Independent Review Panel, UCLF22 Independent Review, 13 February 2023, paras 8.4.2.1–8.4.2.4](https://editorial.uefa.com/resources/027e-174e23083d46-84d25c2e6e55-1000/uclf22_independent_review_report_20230213194627.pdf)
- [UEFA, special refund scheme for the 2022 Champions League final, 7 March 2023](https://www.uefa.com/news-media/news/027f-1779bb90c5d1-9f13bc061dcd-1000/)

## Your brief

The following scenario, roles, records and deadlines are constructed for teaching.

You chair the review of a fictional European club final after spectators reported missed play and inaccessible assistance. The league owns the event, the landlord contracted the admissions operator, and the two finalist clubs each sold an allocation. A public meeting with supporters takes place in five days. You can commission a review and recommend expenditure to the board.

| Exercise record | What you have received |
| --- | --- |
| What the records cover | Of 600 tickets in the disputed allocation, 420 were club sales and 180 were league sales. A timestamp is missing for 90 tickets. The admissions operator says its export may omit manual admissions. Several households bought tickets in one transaction. |
| Fan liaison account | The disability supporters’ group holds eleven confidential accounts about missed assistance. Some companions hold tickets on separate bookings. The group offers anonymised themes and will pass invitations to its members. |
| Decision and custody | The clubs have purchaser contacts for their sales. The landlord holds the contractor agreement. The league holds the event plan and controls remedy expenditure only up to €10,000 without board approval. |
| Price and scope | All 600 tickets in this exercise cost €35 each. The transport authority has also received complaints about the journey. Its records have not yet been reconciled with the league’s timetable. |

## Decisions due

1. Write the review’s terms of reference and a custody schedule for the records. Identify who must account for the whole event, where each record originated and how supporters can contribute without surrendering personal testimony to the clubs.
2. Compare a cohort refund with an individual claims route. Cost the full cohort, name the officer who can authorise it, and design a route for a companion or manually admitted spectator who cannot produce the expected timestamp.
3. Prepare a two-minute statement for the public meeting. Identify one immediate change, its owner and a date for testing it with supporters before the next event. Say when the board will report on unresolved issues.

## An agent assignment for the event director

Have the event agent join steward returns, accessible arrival reports and sign-offs into a timed record. A blank radio report still calls for a person to check the position. Give the duty director a named route to stop admission and update supporters.
