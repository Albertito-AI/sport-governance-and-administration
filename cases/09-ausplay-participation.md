# A change of survey reaches the grants committee

Chapter case 09 · Chapter 9, *Participation and the local sporting system* · Australia

## The public record

Australia’s AusPlay survey collected participation data by telephone from October 2015 to June 2023. The Australian Sports Commission moved collection online in July 2023 and treats the change as a break in the series. It starts trends in the new series with 2023–24 and identifies a new baseline using 2023–25 data. Its methodological archive provides questionnaires and reports for each period. For a local administrator, the public series is a useful benchmark, provided the denominator, population, period and collection method are named alongside the number being reported.

- [Australian Sports Commission, AusPlay, ‘AusPlay data’](https://www.ausport.gov.au/clearinghouse/research/ausplay)
- [Australian Sports Commission, AusPlay method, separate telephone and online series and technical reports](https://www.ausport.gov.au/clearinghouse/research/ausplay/method)

## Your brief

The following scenario, roles, records and deadlines are constructed for teaching.

In this fictional municipality, a sport-for-development trust wants a second year of council funding. Its chair asks you to approve a claim that low-cost sessions raised participation across the district. The grant condition asks for both repeat engagement among new entrants and evidence of whom the scheme is failing to reach.

| Exercise record | What you have received |
| --- | --- |
| District survey | A telephone survey reported weekly activity by 36% of 500 responding adults last year. An online opt-in survey gives 44% of 620 this year. Recruitment channels and the age mix changed. The trust has no matched sample or bridging study. |
| Programme register | Among 200 unique first-time adult entrants in the spring term, 126 have a logged second visit within eight weeks. Two centres could scan repeat visits into the common register. A third kept anonymous headcounts. |
| Outreach record | The disability access group and the tenants’ network both circulated paper invitations. Neither received the survey link. The trust recorded 70 returnable enquiry cards but did not ask whether their holders later attended. |
| Council timetable | The renewal hearing is in five working days. Council can authorise a one-term extension with conditions. The trust has funding for one part-time worker for six weeks to repair its enrolment and feedback arrangements. |

## Decisions due

1. Redraft the chair’s chart and its caption so readers can see what each figure represents. State which claim about district change the two surveys can sustain and what information you would need for a defensible comparison.
2. Report the eight-week repeat measure with numerator, denominator and a short account of the missing visits. Give the council one usable number now, including its limits, and one way to check coverage without collecting unnecessary identifiers.
3. Recommend a renewal decision and design the six-week repair. Allocate work between centre managers, community partners and the grant officer. Set a review date and identify which proposed investment would change if the returns remain incomplete.

## An agent assignment for the participation manager

Instruct the reporting agent to attach the survey method, denominator and collection dates to every comparison. A change of method should interrupt the dashboard series. Ask clubs with falling response to explain whose participation the new channel might miss.
