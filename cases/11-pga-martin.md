# Who decides whether walking is part of golf?

Chapter case 11 · Chapter 11, *Inclusion, power and community ownership* · United States

## The public record

Professional golfer Casey Martin sought permission to use a cart in tournaments because of a circulatory disability. The Supreme Court of the United States held on 29 May 2001 that the playing areas of the tour’s golf courses came within Title III of the Americans with Disabilities Act and that Martin could seek access as a competitor. Under 42 USC § 12182(b)(2)(A)(ii), the tour had to consider his individual circumstances. The trial court had found that even with a cart he experienced more fatigue than other players experienced from walking. On those facts the modification left the character of the competition intact, and the injunction permitting the cart stood.

- [PGA Tour, Inc v Martin, 532 US 661, 677–91 (2001), majority opinion](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-532/pdf/USREPORTS-532-661.pdf)
- [Americans with Disabilities Act, 42 USC § 12182(b)(2)(A)(ii)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title42/pdf/USCODE-2024-title42-chap126-subchapIII-sec12182.pdf)

## Your brief

The following scenario, roles, records and deadlines are constructed for teaching.

In this fictional transfer exercise, you are tournament director of a US open amateur golf qualifier run by a private operator whose competition falls within Title III. Pairings go out tonight. A qualified player requests a cart for the two qualifying rounds and asks that the organisers keep the medical documentation confidential.

| Exercise record | What you have received |
| --- | --- |
| Competition rule | Players ordinarily walk. The competition committee may approve individual modifications and the referee may adjust the starting order. Every entrant plays the same tees and holes. |
| Player’s information | A clinician confirms that walking the course would prevent the player from finishing. The player can play the full round with transport between shots and offers to discuss practical arrangements privately. |
| Operations note | A vehicle is available on day one. On day two the site operator has assigned its only vehicle to range inspection. A neighbouring course can deliver a replacement for US$180 if booked by 17.00. |
| Players’ representative | Another entrant asks what competitive benefit the vehicle confers and wants the whole field to see the medical file before pairings are released. |

## Decisions due

1. Draft the committee’s ruling. State which feature of the competition the walking rule is meant to preserve, identify the individual evidence and explain your conclusion under the US provision.
2. Write the operations instruction for both days, naming the person who books the second vehicle, the person who checks it and the official who can adjust the draw if delivery fails.
3. Prepare separate notices for the applicant and the rest of the field. Specify what can be explained about the rule and what information the applicant can keep private.

## An agent assignment for the competition committee

Pilot an access agent on the venue’s route, transport and equipment records. It can surface remedies that keep an athlete in the competition. The athlete is entitled to discuss the proposed adjustment with the authorised decision-maker in private.
